# LAMBO CITY + ALIVE — Founders' Club and Luxury World Production Blueprint

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 27 November 2026  
**Status:** Founder-approved creative direction; implementation blueprint; Preview-first  
**Production rule:** This document records direction. It does not authorize changes to protected live systems or production deployment.

## 1. Creative mandate

Build LAMBO CITY to a high-end open-world standard with the resources available to this project. The world must feel authored, responsive, inhabited, cinematic, and exceptionally finished. The target is not to imitate another studio's assets or proprietary presentation. The target is to adopt disciplined production methods: responsive controls, coherent animation, purposeful world simulation, strong art direction, careful optimization, and consistent quality.

**Creative principle:** Luxury is the foundation. Art is the expression. Performance is the engine. ALIVE connects everything.

LAMBO CITY and ALIVE are one connected project with distinct roles:
- **LAMBO CITY:** the destination, social world, entertainment, culture, waterfront, events, and shared experiences.
- **ALIVE:** the personal identity and Companion layer, including privacy, preferences, saved creations, continuity, and future portability.
- **HERO:** founder identity, permanent owner, creative host, and guide for the first invited creative experiences.

HERO's visual design is locked to the founder's original reference boards. No redesign, reinterpretation, restyling, replacement concept image, or unapproved performance change.

## 2. The first launch: Founders' Room / private social club

The first public-facing cohort is intentionally small and invitation-led. The first member should feel personally welcomed into a private creative destination, not pushed through a complicated software registration form.

### First-session experience target
1. **Arrival:** enter LAMBO CITY and understand immediately that this is a curated, private early-member experience.
2. **Welcome:** introduce the world, its etiquette, privacy expectations, and the current preview status in concise, premium language.
3. **Meet HERO:** HERO is the host and guide. His existing appearance, performance choreography, music timing, and owner authority remain protected.
4. **Explore:** allow the invited member to experience the approved public spaces and the specific invitation-only spaces assigned to the cohort.
5. **Create:** offer the guest-first ALIVE Companion experience where available, with clear local-save and data-continuity disclosures.
6. **Return:** make the next visit understandable; do not claim cross-device restoration, account sync, email verification, or cloud backup until each is proven end to end.
7. **Feedback:** collect focused feedback from the first cohort and use it to improve reliability before widening access.

### Access and release principles
- Begin with the founder and invited friends / first members; do not open every ALIVE capability at once.
- Guest-first entry is preferred so a visitor can understand the experience before account setup.
- Local guest saves must be described accurately as local to that device/browser; they are not cloud backup.
- Account claiming, passkeys, cross-device restoration, recovery, export, and deletion must be tested as a complete identity promise before public launch.
- Private areas remain invitation-gated. Owner access for HERO is permanent and cannot depend on reputation or progression.
- The first cohort is a learning group, not a claim that unfinished systems are complete.
- ALIVE has its own staged rollout / white-paper roadmap; LAMBO CITY and ALIVE remain one connected product.

### Current implementation status — do not overstate
- PR #204: guest-first Companion onboarding and optional account claim; open draft, Preview work only. Email/account claim still has Preview configuration and end-to-end test blockers.
- PR #205: ALIVE brand system and friends-first rollout roadmap; open draft, Preview work only.
- PR #203: owner approval safeguard reconciliation; open draft, not merged.
- PR #207: early playable controls and staged world loading; merged and deployed to production.
- The current production release does not include PR #204 or PR #205. Their branches must be reconciled against current main before any integration decision.
- No account, Companion, social graph, voice, cross-device sync, or creator feature may be described as production-ready without a real end-to-end test.

## 3. Three private creative reference pillars

These are internal creative references, not permission to copy logos, monograms, signature products, characters, copyrighted artwork, protected trade dress, or imply affiliation or sponsorship.

### Pillar 01 — Lamborghini: performance, precision, presence
Translate into original LAMBO CITY design through:
- Strong, deliberate silhouettes and proportions.
- Crisp engineering details, accurate alignment, refined edges, and believable mechanical logic.
- Convincing paint, glass, metal, rubber, and reflective surfaces.
- Responsive movement, acceleration, camera feedback, and premium vehicle presentation.
- Cinematic reveals that respect player control and device performance.

### Pillar 02 — Louis Vuitton: craftsmanship, architecture, hospitality
Translate into original LAMBO CITY design through:
- Materials that look and behave as if selected with intention.
- Layered architecture, exact trim, joins, thresholds, handles, frames, lighting, and furniture.
- Curated storefronts, hospitality, member arrival, private spaces, and collectible experiences.
- Tactile variation: polished versus brushed metal, honed versus polished stone, clear versus tinted glass, woven versus smooth surfaces.
- Detail that rewards close inspection without visual clutter.
- Consistency across architecture, interfaces, clothing, event presentation, and member service.

### Pillar 03 — Takashi Murakami: expressive art, color, imagination
Translate into original LAMBO CITY design through:
- Original characters, artwork, clothing treatments, accessories, and collectible art moments.
- Selective bold color and playful contrast against the restrained luxury foundation.
- Curated art zones and artist-configurable surfaces rather than uncontrolled decoration everywhere.
- Original commissions and creator-owned work with clear rights and attribution.
- Expressive details that add personality without making the shared world noisy.

The balance matters: the default city remains sophisticated and believable; art and fashion become deliberate focal points. Users should be able to express themselves without overwhelming the shared environment.

The latest Pharrell Williams Louis Vuitton menswear show found in this research is **Spring-Summer 2027**, presented in Paris on 23 June 2026. Its central idea connected surfing culture, coast, water, travel, and community. The scenography used a monumental breaking-wave backdrop, sand, water, and a boardwalk-like runway. The collection combined relaxed tailoring, technical surf references, weathered materials, hand-woven textures, marine embellishments, beads and shells, and functional construction.

No Louis Vuitton marks, monograms, signature bag patterns, branded surfboards/wetsuits, runway set reproduction, or unlicensed show artwork. We create LAMBO CITY's own coastal identity.

## 5. LAMBO CITY world-detail standard

**Preserve Position, Upgrade Scale & Realism.** Approved geography, object placement, footprints, roads, sidewalks, dock and marina positions, HQ, boardwalk, stage, camera, walking paths, joystick/WASD, and locked interactions remain fixed.

The approved district layout is not up for redesign. Upgrade the existing scene through:
- Material definition and believable roughness / metalness.
- Fine architectural trim, panel reveals, joints, thresholds, door hardware, glazing, and mullions.
- Better proportions and visual depth only within the existing approved footprint.
- Realistic reflections and restrained emissive accents.
- High-quality ground transitions, pavers, curbs, dock materials, and waterfront edge details.
- Thoughtful furniture, planters, lighting fixtures, signage, and art only where explicitly approved.
- Environmental storytelling through purposeful props and activity, not random clutter.
- Consistent material families across connected spaces.

### Coastal material language
Use the existing marina setting as the context. Explore a restrained palette of warm stone, weathered timber, dark marine metal, satin stainless details, tinted glass, deep petrol / sea-glass accents, champagne metal highlights, and selectively sun-worn textiles. Use wave, horizon, and surf-culture references abstractly and originally. Do not add a giant wave, move the boardwalk, change the shoreline, or rebuild the waterfront unless the founder specifically authorizes that separate scope.

### Detail hierarchy
1. **Silhouette and composition:** the building / place reads correctly from a distance.
2. **Primary materials:** glass, stone, timber, metal, concrete, water, fabric.
3. **Construction detail:** seams, joints, reveals, frames, edges, thresholds, hardware.
4. **Lighting and reflection:** subtle, physically plausible highlights and controlled glow.
5. **Human scale:** doors, seating, handrails, retail displays, signage, walkable clearances.
6. **Close-up rewards:** small original art, material variation, carefully placed objects.

Never add detail merely to increase object count. Every addition must improve composition, realism, function, or storytelling.

## 6. Social club experience architecture

The first-member experience should be built from reusable systems rather than one-off scripts:
- Invitation / cohort access state.
- Welcome and orientation flow.
- Member profile and visible identity preferences.
- Guest or member Companion presence, with accurate save-state indicators.
- Friend / invite relationships and consent-aware visibility.
- Event invitations and member-only experiences.
- Proximity communication only with explicit microphone permission and clear audience controls.
- Creator attribution, sharing permissions, and moderation.
- Owner / host tools that remain separate from member permissions.

The first cohort can be small and curated. Do not add a public social feed, open voice, or broad creator marketplace as a prerequisite for the first private club experience.

## 7. Rockstar-level production standards using our actual resources

The goal is a browser-native, high-performance experience—not a wholesale engine replacement.

### Player and camera
- Input responds immediately and consistently.
- Clear idle, walk, run, turn, stop, and transition behavior.
- Natural acceleration without sacrificing control.
- Camera follow is stable, readable, and comfortable.
- Collision and navigation behave consistently on desktop and iPad.
- No unapproved movement-speed changes.

### World and simulation
- NPCs have purposeful behaviors and believable reactions.
- Events create localized activity rather than permanent global load.
- Systems are staged and do not block the player.
- Repeated geometry/materials are reused where appropriate.
- Unused GPU resources are disposed safely.
- Visual quality is preserved while optimizing frame pacing and memory.

### Browser and device budget
- Keep Three.js 0.160.0 and the existing browser-native modular architecture.
- No React, TypeScript, bundler, or new heavy framework.
- No new paid AI or sandbox dependency.
- Preserve the early playable startup and staged world loading deployed in PR #207.
- Measure frame time, load stages, draw calls, texture sizes, and memory before changing them.
- Optimize for real iPad Safari and desktop behavior, not only static code inspection.

### Workstream D — Player experience audit
1. Record baseline input latency, startup stages, frame pacing, draw calls, and memory.
2. Inspect player, input, camera, collision, and animation as a connected system.
3. Identify defects and opportunities without changing movement speed or controls by assumption.
4. Implement one isolated improvement at a time.
